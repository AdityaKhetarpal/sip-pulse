# UX copy: SIP Pulse (ux-copy pass, 17 Nov 2026)

Readers: a recruiter with 30 seconds, and an Indian retail investor who wants a straight answer.
Voice: a careful analyst talking plainly. Exact numbers, no hype, nothing claimed beyond the data.

## Terms (always the same words)
- **Fall month:** a month the Nifty 50 closed 5% or more below the previous month-end. Never "crash" or "correction".
- **Equity inflows:** net money into active equity mutual funds (money in minus money out). Never "investments".
- **SIP:** explained once as "SIP (a fixed monthly investment, set up once and paid automatically)".
- **Locked test:** the test fixed before seeing data. Never "pre-registration" in headlines; the word appears only on Method.
- **Found after the test:** the label for anything exploratory.
- Rupees: "₹32,160 crore", with Indian digit grouping. Months: "Oct 2026".

## Recommended copy

**Opening line (display):** When the Nifty falls, the money keeps coming.

**Opening sub-line:** We checked 91 months of AMFI data, April 2019 to October 2026. Money into equity funds stayed positive
in every one of the 7 months the Nifty 50 fell 5% or more. SIPs (fixed monthly investments) dipped by about 1% the month
after, then kept growing.

**Verdict cards:**
- "Do equity inflows drop the month after a fall? **Not clearly.** They fell a median 13.9%, but with only 7 falls that is
  within chance (90% range −31% to +8.5%)."
- "Do SIPs hold up? **Almost.** A small dip: −1.0% against +1.6% in other months. That's about ₹300 crore."
- "Found after the test: **Indians buy the dip.** Equity inflows rose in the fall month itself in 6 of 7 falls."

**Main chart title:** SIP money rose from ₹8,238 crore to ₹32,160 crore a month, through every fall.
**Hint under charts:** Hover or tap a month to see its numbers.
**Source line:** Source: AMFI monthly reports and SIP data; Nifty 50 from Yahoo Finance. April 2019 to October 2026.

**Primary action:** See every fall → **Secondary:** How the test works

**Footer:** Analysis for education, not investment advice. Not affiliated with AMFI or NSE.

## Rationale
The opening line is true for the pre-registered results and the raw data alike: equity inflows stayed positive in all 7 fall
months, and SIPs dipped only about 1%. The "buy the dip" finding is striking, but it wasn't part of the locked test, so it
gets its own labelled card, not the headline. Verdicts lead with a plain answer ("Not clearly", "Almost") before the numbers,
so an investor gets the answer and a recruiter sees the rigour.

## Localization notes
"Crore" and Indian digit grouping are deliberate (the audience is Indian). "Buy the dip" is market slang, so it is
explained in the sentence that follows it.
